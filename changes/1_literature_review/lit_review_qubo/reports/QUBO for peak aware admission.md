# Solve the peak LP first, anneal second

## Executive summary

**Recommendation: make an LP/MILP pipeline (HiGHS LP, round-and-repair, optional MILP polish) PeakBaleen's primary episode selector. Keep the QUBO/Ising route (simulated bifurcation on GPU, simulated annealing on CPU) as the H2 comparison arm on a pegged sub-problem, never as the source of the labels.** Four findings support this. First, the problem has a structure that classical solvers exploit very well. As a MILP it has n ≈ 10^4–10^6 binary columns but only m+1 ≈ 145–1001 rows. A vertex of its LP relaxation therefore has at most about m fractional variables, so rounding costs only a small, certified additive gap (our derivation from the standard vertex argument). Second, the evidence on constrained knapsack-type problems runs against Ising solvers. Raw penalty-QUBO outputs were "completely inferior to the greedy method" on the quadratic knapsack problem, and even after repair they trailed Gurobi's mean gap (0.172% vs 0.075%) ([Ohno et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.19175v1)). Third, a dense QUBO of our instance cannot even be stored: the budget penalty alone has about 5×10^9 couplers at n = 10^5. On one 24 GB GPU, the open-source SB package tops out near n ≈ 5×10^4 dense in fp32. Fourth, the real novelty and the real risk both sit in the *formulation*, not the solver. Daniel Wong's CMU thesis (Ch. 7) already tried peak-aware episode scoring and got a 29% Peak-DT gain in its analytical model that "we could not translate … over to simulation" ([Wong thesis](http://reports-archive.adm.cs.cmu.edu/anon/2024/CMU-CS-24-152.pdf)). That same thesis names per-10-minute DT accounting as future work (§7.4.3), which is exactly our d_{e,w}. PeakBaleen should therefore frame itself as the first global min-max selection over all windows, with a certified optimality gap, a solver comparison in which Ising is an honest competitor, and a measured distillation retention for H3. It should not frame itself as "a QUBO method for caching". Under the frozen scope, H3 is expected to lose much of the gain. Nothing in the imitation-learning literature suggests a time-blind GBM can recover labels driven by time-of-day, so H3 should be treated as a measurement with a pre-computed Bayes ceiling.

## The problem is a max-min knapsack with 1,000 rows, which favours LP

PeakBaleen's swap block is one new `Policy` subclass. It returns an ordering whose prefix at the training budget W = 35.599 MB/s is exactly the selected set, and every downstream component (episode model, GBM, features, the `threshold < target_wr` label rule, the simulator) is frozen (SCOPE.md §1–3). The optimization is (P): minimize z subject to Σ_e d_{e,w} x_e + z ≥ C_w for every window w, Σ_e s_e x_e ≤ W, and x ∈ {0,1}^n. The instance has roughly 144 ten-minute windows per trace-day and 10^4–10^6 episodes. Each episode touches only a few consecutive windows, so the constraint matrix has about n·L nonzeros (about 4×10^6 at n = 10^6 with L ≈ 3).

**Complexity rules out worst-case guarantees but not good practice.** With each window treated as a scenario, (P) is a discrete max-min multi-scenario knapsack with per-scenario offsets C_w. When the number of scenarios is part of the input, min-max knapsack is **strongly NP-hard and not approximable within any factor**. With a constant number of scenarios it is only weakly NP-hard, with a pseudo-polynomial DP and an FPTAS ([Aissi, Bazgan & Vanderpooten survey, EJOR 2009](https://www.lamsade.dauphine.fr/~bazgan/Papers/EJOR09a.pdf)). That DP runs in time exponential in the number of scenarios, so it is useless at m ≥ 144. A search snippet claiming strong NP-hardness even for a fixed number of scenarios ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0305054806002577)) contradicts the survey's pseudo-polynomial DP and should be treated as erroneous. Time-locality does not remove hardness, because a single window is already a knapsack. For a fixed z, and with d constant over each episode's interval, the decision version is a generalization of UFP-cover on a path. UFP-cover has a 4-approximation, (1+ε) results under resource augmentation ([Cristi & Wiese, STACS 2020](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.STACS.2020.44)) and a QPTAS ([Höhn, Mestre & Wiese, ICALP 2014](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-662-43948-7_52)). No source settles the complexity of max-min knapsack when scenario profiles are interval-structured, so that remains open. Because z can be zero or negative, multiplicative ratios are the wrong yardstick anyway. What matters is additive error against the LP bound.

**The LP relaxation is almost integral.** The LP has m+1 non-bound rows, and z is free, so an optimal basic solution has **at most about m fractional x_e**: at most 1,000 out of 10^6, or 0.1%. This follows from the classical vertex argument behind Lenstra–Shmoys–Tardos and the d-dimensional knapsack LP; that attribution is **[from memory, unverified]**, and the bound applied to (P) is our own derivation. Rounding the fractional variables down keeps the budget feasible and raises the peak by at most the d-mass of the fractional episodes in the worst window. This yields a per-instance certificate z_LP ≤ z_OPT ≤ z_round at no extra cost. The same logic underpins the LP-based PTAS for fixed-dimensional knapsack ([FSTTCS 2022](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol250-fsttcs2022/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2022.23/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2022.23.pdf)) and the recent PTAS for covering MILPs with a fixed number of constraints ([Grobben, Moura & Yaman, arXiv:2512.02571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02571)). The bound loosens only if a few "lumpy" episodes carry d_{e,w} comparable to C_w. The Frieze–Clarke remedy is to enumerate or branch on those large episodes first. The dedicated max-min knapsack literature (surrogate relaxation with pegging, which reports "upper and lower bounds of very high accuracy in a few seconds" ([Taniguchi, Yamada & Kataoka, C&OR 2008](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1016/j.cor.2006.10.002), search-snippet), and column generation with B&B ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/276366728_Solving_the_Multiscenario_Max-Min_Knapsack_Problem_Exactly_with_Column_Generation_and_Branch-and-Bound), authors unverified)) confirms that classical bounding works well on this problem family.

**Dualizing the budget alone does not decompose the problem.** Dualizing only the budget leaves the max over windows coupling every episode. The decomposing relaxation dualizes the *window* rows with simplex multipliers μ_w. That turns (P) into one 0/1 knapsack with profits p_e = Σ_w μ_w d_{e,w}. Adding a budget price λ then gives the per-episode threshold rule x_e = 1 iff Σ_w μ_w d_{e,w} > λ s_e. The best μ and λ are exactly the LP duals. With only about 1,000 rows, solving the LP directly is simpler than hand-rolled subgradient and at least as tight. The rule is still worth computing, because it gives an interpretable per-episode "peak value per byte" score that can populate the `scores` output.

### Recommended primary pipeline (fits the frozen swap block)

The pipeline is ordered by payoff per engineering hour. None of the solver timings has been benchmarked on our instances, so a pilot on sample 0 is the first task.

| Step | What | Tooling | Expected cost (estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Build sparse D (n × m) by splitting each episode's `service_time_saved` across 10-min windows by its access timestamps. Build C_w as the per-window no-admission DT and s_e from `chunks_written`, using the same unit the harness converts into cumulative write rate. | numpy/scipy.sparse | minutes |
| 1 | Solve the LP relaxation of (P) with dual simplex or IPM. Record z_LP and the duals μ, λ. | `highspy` (HiGHS 1.15) or OR-Tools MathOpt | seconds to about a minute at n = 10^6 **[unbenchmarked]** |
| 2 | Fix integral values, round the ≤ m+1 fractional ones down, then fill the residual budget greedily by marginal peak reduction per byte, or solve the ≤ ~1,000-binary residual as a MILP. Report z_round − z_LP. | HiGHS MILP | seconds |
| 3 | **Lexicographic stage.** Constrain z ≤ z* + ε and maximize Σ_e dt_e x_e (Baleen's own DT-saved) with the leftover budget. | HiGHS LP/MILP | seconds |
| 4 | Optional MILP polish: time limit plus 0.1% gap target, warm-started from step 3, after LP reduced-cost pegging. | HiGHS, Gurobi (academic) or COPT | minutes; proven optimality may never arrive because of symmetric plateaus |
| 5 | Emit the order: selected set first, sorted by the λ-adjusted dual score, then the rest in Baleen's order. Assert that the harness prefix at W reproduces the selection exactly. | — | — |

Step 3 is not cosmetic. A pure min-max objective is flat everywhere except at the argmax window. Without a secondary objective, the solver can leave budget unused or spend it arbitrarily off-peak, which would degrade mean DT and scramble the labels H3 depends on. A CVaR-style variant that minimizes the mean of the top-k windows is also LP-representable. It is a cheap robustness ablation against the peak-migration failure described below; this suggestion is our inference, not a sourced result. Among solvers, the July 2026 Mittelmann MIPLIB run shows open-source HiGHS 1.15.1 (parallel) solving 179/240 instances against COPT 8.0.3's 219/240, and Gurobi is absent because it withdrew from the benchmark ([Mittelmann](https://plato.asu.edu/ftp/milp.html)). HiGHS is therefore adequate for a problem this well-conditioned. PDLP is built for LPs with 10^8–10^10 nonzeros ([Applegate et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07018)), and its modest accuracy would blur the vertex structure that rounding relies on, so it is unnecessary here. CP-SAT's integer-coefficient model is likely weaker than LP-based MILP on 10^6 Booleans with long weighted sums **[inference, unverified]**.

## A QUBO encoding needs four compromises and a matrix-free solver

The QUBO arm is scientifically legitimate as H2 ("solver comparison on a fixed formulation", SCOPE.md §4). It has to be built carefully, because each standard encoding choice interacts badly with our instance.

**The knapsack constraint.** There are four families. Slack with a log/binary expansion is exact and costs only ⌈log2(W/Δs)⌉ ≈ 20–40 extra spins. It still makes the constraint fully dense, and correctness needs 0 < B·max(c) < A, which widens the dynamic range ([Lucas 2014](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.5843)). A plain λ(Σ s_e x_e − W)^2 is an *equality*, so it biases toward using exactly W and usually overshoots slightly. Unbalanced penalization, −λ1·h + λ2·h^2 with h = W − Σ s_e x_e, needs no slack and beat slack on bin packing (29 vs 11 items solved on D-Wave Hybrid) ([Montañez-Barrera et al., QST 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13914)). Its minimum sits at h* = λ1/(2λ2), which is a soft margin rather than an exact inequality, and its published λ values (0.9603, 0.0371) were tuned on a 13-item instance, so they must be rescaled to our units. The coupling λ2·s_e s_e' also stays dense. Six translations of the QKP constraint were compared under SA, and the best one used no auxiliary variables ([Bontekoe et al., ICCS 2023](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-36030-5_8), abstract only). The fourth family, which **we recommend**, moves the budget out of the QUBO as a Lagrangian term μ·Σ s_e x_e. That term changes only the diagonal, and μ is updated by bisection or subgradient against the observed write total. This is the Hubbard–Stratonovich or dual approach ([Ohzeki, Sci. Rep. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.05298); inequality extension, [Takabayashi, Goto & Ohzeki](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06901)). In one scheduling case it cut the physical qubits needed from 2,499 to 320 ([Yu & Nabil, Front. Phys. 2021](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physics/articles/10.3389/fphy.2021.730685/full)). If a penalty is used anyway, Glover et al. suggest starting P at **75–150% of an estimate of the objective value** and then re-solving with feasibility checks ([Glover, Kochenberger & Du](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.11538)). No source calibrates that rule for sum-of-squares objectives. The failure modes are well documented. If the weight is too small, solutions are infeasible. If it is too large, solvers return "feasible states that may be far from optimal" ([Alessandroni et al., 2026](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02416)).

**The min-max objective.** The only exact route is an epigraph. A K-bit peak variable t and a K-bit slack σ_w per window enforce C_w − Σ_e d_{e,w} x_e + σ_w = t under penalty A, with B·t minimized, following the pattern of Lucas's job-sequencing Hamiltonian ([Lucas §6.3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.5843)). For m = 144 and K ≈ 10–12, this adds only about 1.7k spins. However, t appears in every window's square, so it couples to every episode and creates a hub, and A ≫ B pushes the dynamic range up. There is no published scaling evidence for this form at 10^5 variables, so we recommend it as an ablation only. Scheduling practitioners instead state that "the maximum function cannot be directly encoded in a QUBO". They either replace it with a balancing (sum-of-squares) proxy ([arXiv:2607.13325](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.13325), **[not full-text verified]**) or solve a decision version with a fixed horizon and bisect on it ([Venturelli, Marchand & Rojo](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.08479)). The surrogate Σ_w α_w L_w^2, with α reweighted toward the argmax window, is Lawson's IRLS for Chebyshev approximation. It converges linearly only for *continuous linear* problems ([arXiv:2401.00778](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.00778); [Nakatsukasa & Trefethen](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06001)), and no guarantee exists for binaries. Log-sum-exp is not quadratic, but its softmax gradient (α_w ∝ exp(βL_w)) can serve as the reweighting rule. The notes found no QUBO paper that uses it.

**Recommended QUBO for our instance (H2 arm).** This is our synthesis, and each element is an inference built on the sources above.

| Element | Choice | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Variables | Only the n_Q "contested" episodes: those not pegged by LP reduced costs *and* touching an active window A = {w : L_w(x_LP) ≥ z_LP − δ}. Target n_Q ≤ 5×10^4. | Makes a dense J fit on 24 GB; pegging is the classical analogue of persistency. |
| Objective | E(x) = Σ_{w∈A} α_w (L_w(x) − τ)^2 + μ Σ_e s̃_e x_e | Balancing surrogate; budget handled as a linear dual term. |
| Target τ | Set just below the best feasible peak (bisect, Venturelli-style) | Limits the surrogate's perverse penalty on windows that over-save (L_w < τ). |
| α, μ init | From the LP window duals and the budget dual λ | Warm start; makes the comparison conservative for Ising. |
| α update | Lawson multiplicative or softmax (bounded β), floor/cap α ∈ [ε, 1] | Stops weight collapse and the dynamic-range blow-up. |
| Scaling | s̃ = s/W; d̃ = d / max_w C_w; optionally quantize to a few bits | Dynamic range governs robustness ([Mücke et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.02195)). |
| Stopping | Track the best *true* max_w L_w after repair, not the surrogate | No convergence guarantee for binary IRLS. |
| Post-processing | Greedy repair (drop worst peak-per-byte until feasible), greedy fill, 1-flip/swap local search | Mandatory: raw outputs lose to greedy on QKP. |
| Ablations | Exact epigraph QUBO; unbalanced-penalty budget; slack-encoded budget | Shows how much the encoding choice costs. |

**Density decides feasibility.** Q_{ee'} = Σ_w α_w d_{e,w} d_{e',w} (+ λ s_e s_e' if the budget is a penalty) is a rank-≤m matrix plus a rank-1 matrix. At n = 10^6 over one trace-day there are about 20k episodes per window, which gives a 2×10^8-coupler clique per window and about 3×10^10 in total. That cannot be stored. SB needs only J·x per step, though, and J·x = Dᵀ(α ⊙ Dx) + λ s (sᵀx) costs O(nnz(D)). A custom matrix-free SB in PyTorch (about 100 lines) would therefore scale to the full instance. This is the low-rank structure that spatial-photonic Ising machines are designed around ([arXiv:2303.14993](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.14993); [arXiv:2406.01400](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.01400)). The notes found no published SB implementation of this kind with a knapsack term, so it would be engineering on our side, and the off-the-shelf package documents no sparse path **[UNVERIFIED]**.

**The competitiveness evidence splits cleanly by problem type.** On unconstrained, dense MAX-CUT and spin-glass problems, SB is strong. It solved a 100k-node all-to-all MAX-CUT about 10× faster than the authors' best SA ([Goto et al., Sci. Adv. 2019](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/332535366_Combinatorial_optimization_by_simulating_adiabatic_bifurcations_in_nonlinear_Hamiltonian_systems), via summary), and single-GPU SB beat Gurobi on modularity QUBOs ([Li et al., SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5496392), snippet; Gurobi's time limit not checked). On constrained knapsack-type problems the picture reverses. In QKP with n = 1,000–2,000, raw Ising-machine outputs lost to greedy. Even with repair and improvement, the Ising method (62/80 best-known, mean gap 0.172%) trailed Gurobi at 60 s (57/80, **0.075%**) and GRASP+Tabu (**75/80**, 0.121%) ([Ohno, Shirai & Togawa 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.19175v1)). In QKP with 200–300 items, the penalty QUBO solved only **35 of 60** instances. A native-constraint Extended Ising Machine solved all of them, because in a QUBO "flipping any variable from a feasible solution results in an immediate constraint violation" ([Akishima, Tamura & Kudo 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06909)). Benchmarks of hardware and quantum-inspired machines find that no single machine dominates ([Oshiyama & Ohzeki, Sci. Rep. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.14096)), and that annealers "lose their utility when facing problems in practical size and settings" ([Huang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.02325)). Even a quantum-methods paper concedes that Gurobi, CPLEX and CP-SAT "routinely solve problems with thousands of variables to provable optimality" in the QKP/MKP setting ([arXiv:2503.22325](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.22325v1)). Our objective is *linear* with a min-max, and our constraints are few and hard. That is the regime where MILP is strongest, whereas Ising solvers do best on genuinely quadratic, dense objectives.

**Decomposition does not rescue QUBO at this scale.** QBSolv was validated only on instances of about 2,500 variables, with subQUBOs of about 45 variables and a *classical* tabu subsolver ([QBSolv tech report](https://www.dwavequantum.com/media/jhlpvult/partitioning_qubos_for_quantum_acceleration-2.pdf)), and it has been deprecated since 2021/22 ([GitHub](https://github.com/dwavesystems/qbsolv)). dwave-hybrid's Kerberos decomposes by energy impact ([source](https://github.com/dwavesystems/dwave-hybrid/blob/master/hybrid/reference/kerberos.py)), but the shared budget residual and the hub formed by the epigraph t bits defeat locality-based partitioning. Roof-duality persistency (PrePro) achieved 100% reduction on sparse graph problems up to 500k vertices ([Boros, Hammer & Tavares](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Preprocessing-of-unconstrained-quadratic-binary-Boros-Hammer/e589f4a3f298bb0522a33628b0d0b0a4c6133c8d), snippet). The notes found no evidence of high fix rates on penalty-encoded knapsack QUBOs; LP reduced-cost pegging is the cheaper and stronger analogue here. The one decomposition that fits our structure is time-block rolling horizon or LNS, with the budget split across blocks by the LP dual price λ. Once the problem is blocked this way, each block is a small MILP, and a QUBO sub-solver adds nothing except as a comparison.

**H2 protocol.** All arms solve the same instance (P) on sample 0, then samples 0–9. The arms are the Baleen greedy ratio, LP-round, MILP-polish, SB (bSB/dSB/heated, multi-agent) and SA/Tabu on the identical QUBO, all followed by the same repair routine. Report the true peak max_w L_w, the gap to z_LP, the raw-output feasibility rate before repair, wall-clock time on the stated hardware, and budget use. Normalizing by time on identical hardware (one GPU vs 16 cores) fills a gap the literature leaves open.

## Prior art: the QUBO claim is open, the peak claim is not

| Claim | Verdict | Confidence | Must cite / caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| First QUBO/Ising formulation of budget-constrained **flash-cache admission** | Likely holds | ~80% | QUBO already exists for wireless cache placement ([Zhang et al., arXiv:2312.14448](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.14448); TNSE 2024 venue [partly UNVERIFIED]), data allocation ([Q-Data'25](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3736393.3736692)) and index selection ([Q-Data'24](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3665225.3665445)). Never write "first QUBO for caching". |
| First **min-max peak-optimizing** admission | Only with careful wording | ~75% for "first published, successful in simulation"; ~0% for "first attempt" | Wong thesis Ch. 7 attempted it and failed in simulation; Baleen §3.1/§5.6 defer it |
| First Ising-derived labels distilled into an online admission model | Likely holds | ~80% | Loses its force if the labels come from the LP (our recommendation). OPT-imitation learners (Baleen, LRB, Flashield) are the analogues. |
| First QUBO encoding of min-max over time windows (in caching) | No QUBO work found | — | The scheduling makespan QUBO (Lucas; balancing proxies) is the technical precedent |

**Wong's thesis is the decisive prior art.** Baleen explicitly defers peak optimization: "As explicitly optimizing admission for the peak introduces significant complexity, we leave that for future work". Its §5.6 "admit only during high load" extension saved flash writes but "did not reduce Peak DT" ([Baleen, FAST'24](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/fast24-wong.pdf)). The thesis went further ([CMU-CS-24-152](http://reports-archive.adm.cs.cmu.edu/anon/2024/CMU-CS-24-152.pdf)). Its load-dependent thresholds on OPT (§7.4.1) cut flash writes 8.0% but Peak DT only 1.4% on Region3. In §7.4.2, it reweighted episodes that overlap a *fixed* peak period and rescored them by peak-period DT. This PeakOPT cut Peak DT **29% in the analytical model**, but "we could not translate these savings over to simulation": OPT-PeakDT produced "a new, much higher peak appearing in a different part of the trace". The thesis concludes that "a more complex solution would be required" (§7.5), and its §7.4.3 future work proposes dividing the trace "into smaller intervals (e.g., 10 minutes) and calculating the Disk-head Time saved per interval from each admitted episode". That is precisely our d_{e,w}. The thesis thus both scoops the naive idea and names the two failure modes PeakBaleen must beat: optimizing a fixed window invites peak migration, and gains in the analytical model do not survive simulation. A min-max over *all* windows addresses the first by construction. The second is H1's real test, and nothing in the literature guarantees we pass it.

**Other flash-admission work optimizes aggregates.** CacheSack solves a (fractional) knapsack over traffic categories for TCO ([ATC'22](https://www.usenix.org/conference/atc22/presentation/yang-tzu-wei); [TOS'23](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3582014)). Lyons, Rangaswami & Xie give a network-flow offline optimum for write-aware admission with additive utility ([TCS 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304397522007678)). Cheng et al. (ATC'16), Flashield, RIPQ, Kangaroo, LRB and Raven optimize hit ratio, byte-miss ratio or writes **[UNVERIFIED this session]**. HALP evaluates and optimizes the peak-hour P95 byte miss ratio, but through the objective, not an offline peak oracle ([HALP, NSDI'23](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/nsdi23-song-zhenyu.pdf)). Coded caching minimizes peak delivery rate in an information-theoretic setting ([Maddah-Ali & Niesen](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1209.5807)). The Baleen-citing works we found (FDP SSDs [arXiv:2503.11665](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.11665), Nemo [arXiv:2603.09605](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.09605)) do not address peak. A manual Google Scholar "cited by" pass on Baleen and the thesis (FAST'25/'26, ATC'25, OSDI'25, EuroSys'26, HotStorage), an IEEE Xplore query ("quantum annealing" OR QUBO OR Ising) AND caching, and a patent search remain **outstanding before any novelty sentence is finalized**.

**Suggested framing.** "Baleen and Wong's thesis identify peak-DT-optimal admission as open, and report that fixed-window reweighting and load thresholds fail in simulation. We give the first global min-max formulation of episode selection with per-10-minute DT accounting, solve it to a certified near-optimum via LP, compare classical and Ising (simulated bifurcation) solvers on it, and measure how much of the offline peak gain survives distillation into Baleen's unchanged model." The formulation, the certificate and the distillation measurement are the contributions. Ising is an evaluated solver, not the headline.

## H3 will be capped by what nine time-blind features can see

Oracle imitation reliably loses part of the oracle's advantage. LRB closes about a quarter of the heuristic-to-Belady gap, and "the remaining gap … is due to our model's prediction error" ([LRB §6.5](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/nsdi20-paper-song.pdf)). Parrot's normalized hit rate ranges from near-oracle to far below it depending on the program ([Parrot](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.16239)). Baleen itself leaves **16%** on the table relative to its OPT, 9% of DT is attributable to late admission, and its GBM shows precision 76.8%, recall 51.9% and F1 0.619 against OPT labels ([Baleen §5.6, Supp. Table 3](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/fast24-wong.pdf)). None of the surveyed papers measures retention on a *peak* metric, and none of LRB, HALP, Baleen, RL-Cache or Flashield uses a time-of-day or load feature. H3 therefore has no empirical prior. A 30–70% retention is a reasonable, not conservative, planning figure (our inference).

**The mechanism predicts heavy loss.** When a teacher uses information the student cannot see, the student learns π_IL(o) = E[π_teacher(S) | f(S)=o]. The privileged information is "marginalized during imitation learning, resulting in an 'imitation gap'" ([ADVISOR, NeurIPS'21](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.12173)). Baleen documented the caching version: first-miss examples "shared identical features … but had different labels", so the model "predicted the most probable label for each feature set (i.e., Bayes Optimal classifier behavior)" and rejected almost everything on first access ([Baleen §5.6](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/fast24-wong.pdf)). Under SCOPE.md the features are metadata plus 1–6 h access counts, with no time or load signal. A peak-driven relabelling can survive only where it shifts p(y=1|x) *across* feature cells, for example if particular namespaces or users drive the peak. Inside a cell it acts as label noise, and re-converging the admission threshold to the write-rate target absorbs it. Two effects will also tend to get worse. Positives, already only 6% ([Baleen §2.4](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/fast24-wong.pdf)), will likely become rarer, and peak-valuable episodes must be admitted *before* the peak on accesses that look ordinary.

**Several literature mitigations fall outside the frozen scope.** Sample weights or soft labels would require editing `train_ap.py` or the `threshold < target_wr` label rule. A time-varying admission threshold would change `mlnew`. A global-load or hour-of-day feature is explicitly removed from H3. DAgger-style on-policy relabelling ([Parrot: +9.8% normalized hit rate](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.16239)) would alter the frozen eviction-age and training driver. All of these can be *described* as future work, but none can be run. What remains in scope is measurement plus one real lever inside the policy:

| In-scope action | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Define R = (PeakDT_ML-Baleen − PeakDT_ML-Peak) / (PeakDT_OPT-Baleen − PeakDT_OPT-Peak) at matched write rate on held-out days, reported next to Baleen's own OPT→ML retention | Gap-closure metric modelled on Parrot's normalized hit rate; compares R to a baseline, not to 1 |
| Bayes-ceiling diagnostic before training: bin day-1 examples by the exact 9-feature vector (or by GBM leaf), then measure the label-flip fraction per bin and the best "admit top-k bins" peak DT | Separates "not learnable from these features" from "the model failed to learn it" |
| Mutual information between an episode's peak-window hit mass and namespace, user, and the 1–6 h counts | Tests whether the metadata carries any peak signal |
| Label-agreement proxy (LRB's "good decision ratio" idea) between ML decisions and peak-OPT labels | Cheap offline proxy before full simulation |
| **Learnability-aware tie-breaking:** among near-optimal min-max solutions (z ≤ z* + ε), prefer changes to Baleen's label set that land in high-purity feature cells, or minimize label changes (step 3 of the pipeline) | The only lever that can raise R without touching frozen code; our inference, untested |

A negative H3 is a publishable, expected outcome under SCOPE.md §4. The Bayes-ceiling diagnostic lets the paper state *why* it failed.

## Risks and how to retire them

The largest risk is **analytic-to-simulation slippage**, the exact failure of Wong's PeakOPT. Our d_{e,w} assumes that DT savings are additive and independent across episodes. The simulator's eviction interactions, prefetching and late admissions break that assumption, and the argmax window can migrate. Mitigations are the all-window min-max, the top-k/CVaR variant, and an early check: after OPT-mode simulation, compare the per-window DT the simulator reports against C_w − Σ d x on sample 0. Second, the LP and MILP timings at n = 10^6 are estimates from structure, not benchmarks, so a pilot is needed. Third, **unit mismatch** between our s_e and the harness's cumulative write-rate threshold would make the prefix rule cut a different set. Assert exact reproduction of the selection. Fourth, the Ising arm will probably lose. That is a legitimate H2 result, provided the comparison is fair (same instance, same repair, same wall-clock budget, LP bound reported). The notes found no study of SB feasibility rates on penalty QUBOs and no tolerance figures for SB dynamic range. Fifth, **min-max degeneracy** can yield labels that are arbitrary off the peak. The lexicographic stage is mandatory. Sixth, the residual scoop risk (about 20–25%) sits in unindexed IEEE/MDPI edge-caching papers, patents, and 2025–26 Baleen citers. Finally, H3 has no literature prior, and the frozen scope removes every standard remedy. Budget analysis time for the diagnostics rather than for tuning.

## Conclusion

The choice between QUBO and classical methods resolves on structure, not on solver fashion. The problem has about 1,000 coupling rows against up to a million columns, which makes its LP nearly integral, gives it a free optimality certificate, and yields dual prices that are themselves a readable "peak value per byte" rule. An Ising machine cannot match those properties, and on this problem class it cannot yet match the solution quality either. Our derivation reverses the usual framing: the right research question is whether the offline min-max optimum survives the simulator and the frozen GBM, not whether an Ising solver can find it. The thesis showed that gains in the analytical model evaporated. PeakBaleen's defensible contribution is to show, with a certified optimum in hand, where along the chain from formulation to simulation to distillation the peak gain survives or disappears, with SB and SA as honest comparators rather than the load-bearing method.
