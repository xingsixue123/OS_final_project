# Hybrid classical+Ising and learned/neural CO solvers for budget-constrained min-max (peak) selection

Context anchor (our own measurement, not literature): the synthetic CPU audit (`ising_followup/audit/AUDIT.md`) found LP round-down + repair at 9.8 / 6.9 / 1.1 / 0.2 % of headroom (greedy − z_LP) at n = 1k / 10k / 50k / 200k, HiGHS MILP at 1.9 / 0.8 / 1.1 % (no incumbent at 200k in 300 s), true-objective SA from LP (`ls_lp`) at 3.4 / 13.8 / 2.0 / 0.5 %, and the best Ising arm at 10.5 % (dwave_sa, 1k), 10.0 % (openjij_sa, 10k), 10.6 % (matrix-free SB, 50k) and 8.3 % (mfSB, 200k). Only ≤ 26 LP variables are fractional (145 rows). So the remaining gap to z_LP comes from a small combinatorial core plus integrality, which is where any hybrid or learned method has to earn its keep.

## Q1. Which hybrid Ising schemes have published code and reported wins over pure classical on related problems?

### Takeaway
Hybrid "Ising-inside-classical" schemes (LNS with Ising sub-solvers, Ising-driven decomposition feeding Gurobi, D-Wave-style energy-impact decomposition) do report wins, but only over **naive baselines**: the full QUBO sent directly to an Ising machine, or plain Gurobi on a very large monolithic MILP. I found no primary source where such a hybrid beats a tuned LP/MILP-based heuristic on makespan, load balancing, number partitioning, knapsack-with-conflicts or peak minimization. The only open-source, CPU-runnable hybrid framework with LNS-style decomposers is D-Wave's `dwave-hybrid` (Apache-2.0), and it has no min-max-specific win.

### Cited Findings
- **Ising-machine-assisted LNS with tunable subproblem size (LNS-VT)** (Fujimoto, Yamashita, Tanaka, Keio; arXiv 2607.05169, 2026). Each LNS step picks V′ vehicles × T′ consecutive route steps, builds a sub-QUBO with the rest fixed, solves it on Fixstars Amplify AE (10 s per subproblem, 100 iterations), and reinserts the candidate only if it is feasible and improves the objective. On VRP (300 sites, 5 vehicles) the objective is about 10 % better than the older LNS-V after 100 iterations, and LNS-V's final quality is reached with about 30 % fewer iterations. The best subproblem size was 6,000–10,000 variables. On the quadratic multiple knapsack problem (200 items, 5 knapsacks) LNS beat solving the full QUBO directly. The baselines were the naive full QUBO and LNS-V, **not Gurobi**. No code release is mentioned. — [arXiv 2607.05169](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05169)
- A companion paper from the same group studies the geometric characteristics of subproblems in Ising-machine-assisted LNS. — [arXiv 2607.05014](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05014) (I did not read it in full; content [UNVERIFIED])
- **Ising-machine search-space decomposition + parallel Gurobi** (Kawase, Kikuchi, Tamai, Tanaka; arXiv 2602.23038 / Scientific Reports 2026). An Ising solver partitions a CVRP into weakly coupled subproblems through a constrained max-cut, and Gurobi then solves each subproblem in parallel. Reported: 100 % feasibility on instances where plain Gurobi found none; the 30-minute Gurobi quality reached in about 1 minute; the gap to best-known reduced by 3–50 points (e.g., 65.84 % → 8.16 %); 74.65–95.32 % fewer variables. Code is not released (data "on request"). — [arXiv 2602.23038](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.23038); [Sci. Rep.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-57443-z)
- A related search snippet reports a hybrid facility-location framework (100 facilities × 1000 customers) at a 0.2 % gap in 132.7 s against Gurobi's 213.0 s to proven optimality, a 38 % runtime cut. I could not match it to a specific paper, so treat it as [UNVERIFIED]. — [search result pointing to arXiv 2602.23038 / OSTI](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.23038)
- **dwave-hybrid** (Apache-2.0). It provides composable workflows (`EnergyImpactDecomposer`, `InterruptableTabuSampler`, `RacingBranches`, `LoopUntilNoImprovement`, `ArgMin`). The QPU sub-sampler can be swapped for classical tabu or SA, so it runs fully offline. This is the Kerberos-style "decompose by energy impact, solve the sub-QUBO, merge, iterate" loop. — [dwave-hybrid GitHub](https://github.com/dwavesystems/dwave-hybrid)
- **Makespan in QUBO is structurally awkward.** Makespan is not binary, so QUBO formulations need a time-indexed model with a guessed "pseudo-makespan" threshold, or a dummy job that penalizes jobs finishing late. Master/sub-problem decompositions have been proposed to work around this. — [Quantum algorithms for scheduling problems: a survey, EPJ Quantum Technology](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1140/epjqt/s40507-026-00494-y)
- **Transport-robot scheduling (a makespan-type problem): D-Wave hybrid (LBQM), Fujitsu Digital Annealer and Gurobi (3600 s cap).** The abstract reports "promising results for the digital annealer and some opportunities for the hybrid quantum annealer in direct comparison with Gurobi". I did not extract the numbers, so any specific win is [UNVERIFIED]. — [arXiv 2309.09736](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.09736); [Sci. Rep. 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-45668-1)

### Inferences
- Our τ-threshold QUBO, E = Σ_w α_w(C_w − τ − (Dᵀx)_w)² + μ sᵀx, plus the outer reweighting of α and τ, is already the "pseudo-makespan threshold + iterative reweighting" scheme from the scheduling-QUBO literature. The literature offers no better-founded min-max encoding.
- The published hybrid wins all come from **size reduction**: the Ising machine or LNS never faces the full problem. In our problem LP already reduces the problem to ≤ 26 fractional variables. The natural hybrid is therefore "LP → fix integral variables → exact or Ising search on a small contested core". At that size (~10²–10³ variables) exact MILP on the core (HiGHS) is a stronger sub-solver than SA/SB. The Ising sub-solver only becomes attractive when the sub-objective has quadratic interactions that LP/MILP cannot express linearly (e.g., per-slot cache-capacity coupling, pairwise prefetch interference).

### Gaps
- I found no primary source reporting an Ising/QUBO hybrid beating Gurobi, HiGHS, CP-SAT or a tuned LP heuristic on makespan, load balancing, number partitioning, knapsack-with-conflicts or peak minimization under equal wall-clock. The Keio group's comparisons are against naive full-QUBO or monolithic Gurobi.
- I did not find branch-and-bound with QUBO primal heuristics (QUBO used as the B&B primal heuristic), or time-block decomposition specifically for min-max peak problems, in a primary source with code.
- The transport-robot paper's per-instance numbers were not extracted.

## Q2. Which learned or neural solvers accept arbitrary differentiable or QUBO energies with constraints, run on CPU at 10⁴–10⁵ variables, and how good are they against Gurobi or greedy?

### Takeaway
Two instance-wise, "dataless" relaxation-annealing solvers fit the problem: **FEM** (Nature Comp. Sci. 2025) and **QQA** (ICLR 2025). Both minimize a user-supplied energy by gradient descent over relaxed marginals, so they can take a smooth-max peak objective with a budget penalty, and both are PyTorch and run on CPU. QQA is BSD-3 with native resource-constraint support; FEM's license was not visible. **PI-GNN** (MIT-0 code) and **dQNN** are the same idea, with a GNN or a quadratic network as the parameterization. Supervised or trained-distribution solvers (**DIFUSCO, T2T/Fast-T2T, GFlowNet-CO, Erdős Goes Neural**) need training on an instance distribution with a fixed problem family (TSP/MIS/MaxCut/MCut). They do not naturally accept our custom min-max and budget energy, and they are GPU-oriented. The critiques (Angelini & Ricci-Tersenghi; Boettcher; FrontierCO, ICLR 2026) show that learned solvers often lose to simple greedy or classical solvers, especially at large scale.

### Cited Findings
**FEM, Free-Energy Machine** (Shen, Pan, Wang et al., Nat. Comput. Sci. 5:322–332, 2025; arXiv 2412.09285)
- Method: minimize a variational mean-field free energy F_MF = E[energy under marginals] − (1/β)·entropy. Marginals are softmax-parameterized, β is annealed from high to low temperature, many replicas run in parallel, the optimizer is Adam, and gradients come from autograd or explicit formulas (explicit is about 50 % faster). Constraints are soft penalties (e.g., the imbalance penalty λ in balanced min-cut). — [arXiv HTML 2412.09285](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.09285); [Nature Comp. Sci.](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-025-00782-0)
- Results: beats discrete SB (dSBM) on G-set MaxCut (800–2000 nodes); lower cuts than METIS and KaFFPaE on balanced min-cut with q = 2–32; optimal on 448/454 MaxSAT-2016 instances, outperforming the fastest incomplete MaxSAT solvers from the competition; scaled to 10⁶-node random graphs with R = 50 replicas; 22–27 % lower edge-cut than METIS on a 1,495,802-operator chip-partitioning task. The GPU is about 10× faster than the CPU, and on CPU MaxSAT instances take "a few seconds on average". — [arXiv HTML 2412.09285](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.09285)
- Code: https://github.com/Fanerst/FEM. It covers MaxCut, balanced min-cut, Max-k-SAT, vertex cover and "QUBO-like" instances, and supports custom problems "as long as the expectation value of the target function can be written as some function of the marginal probability". Setup is a conda environment plus a manual PyTorch install. **No license was stated on the README page I fetched, so treat it as [UNVERIFIED] or all-rights-reserved until checked.** — [FEM GitHub](https://github.com/Fanerst/FEM)
- The Nature Comp. Sci. News & Views frames it as "integrating statistical physics and machine learning for CO". — [N&V](https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-025-00794-w)
- FEM is **not compared against Gurobi or an LP heuristic** in what I extracted. Its baselines are dSBM, METIS/KaFFPaE and MaxSAT solvers.

**QQA / PQQA, Parallel Quasi-Quantum Annealing** (Ichikawa & Arai, ICLR 2025; arXiv 2409.02135)
- Method: relax x to σ(w) ∈ [0,1]^N and minimize l̂(σ) + γ·Σ_i(1 − (2σ_i − 1)^α). γ is annealed from negative (convex, minimized at ½, analogous to a strong transverse field) to positive (drives σ toward {0,1}). Parallel chains are coupled by a diversity bonus −S·α·Σ_i STD_s[σ_i^(s)]. Constraints are penalty terms λ_i v_i(x). — [arXiv HTML 2409.02135](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.02135); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=9EfBeXaXf0)
- Benchmarks: MIS, max clique, MaxCut, balanced graph partition and graph coloring, against iSCO, KaMIS, Gurobi (1-hour limit), PI-GNN variants, DIMES, SA and hMETIS, on a V100 GPU with 100–1000 chains and 3k–30k steps. On 10⁶-node random regular graphs it reports approximation ratio 0.980 in 48 s, against 0.695 in 30 min for SA. The abstract claims "superior speed-quality trade-offs for large-scale instances compared to iSCO, learning-based solvers, commercial solvers, and specialized algorithms". I also extracted an Erdős–Rényi (9k–11k nodes) comparison of PQQA ApR 1.043 in ~6.8 h against iSCO 1.008 in 1.25 h; the units and direction of that ratio are unclear from the extraction, so treat it as [UNVERIFIED]. — [arXiv 2409.02135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.02135)
- Code: https://github.com/Yuma-Ichikawa/QQA4CO, **BSD-3-Clause**. The current README claims device "auto" (CPU or CUDA), MPS/LP/QPLIB/QUBO input, a black-box callable objective, native cardinality, cumulative-resource, SOS and indicator constraints handled with per-constraint augmented-Lagrangian multipliers, "feasibility-first archives, and explicit repair", and a benchmark protocol against SCIP on MIPLIB/QPLIB under equal wall-clock. **These features appear to post-date the ICLR paper; I have not checked the benchmark numbers, so treat them as [UNVERIFIED].** — [QQA4CO GitHub](https://github.com/Yuma-Ichikawa/QQA4CO)

**PI-GNN** (Schuetz, Brubaker, Katzgraber, Nat. Mach. Intell. 2022)
- An unsupervised GNN outputs per-node probabilities and is trained on the relaxed QUBO Hamiltonian of a single instance. The official example (amazon-science/co-with-gnns-example) covers MIS, uses DGL + PyTorch, runs on a laptop CPU in about 30–60 s, and is **MIT-0** (docs CC-BY-SA-4.0). — [co-with-gnns-example](https://github.com/amazon-science/co-with-gnns-example)
- There is also a JAX/Flax reimplementation. — [IvanIsCoding/GNN-for-Combinatorial-Optimization](https://github.com/IvanIsCoding/GNN-for-Combinatorial-Optimization)

**Dataless quadratic NN (dQNN)** (Alkhouri et al., arXiv 2406.19532; earlier Neural Networks 155:168–176, 2022)
- Encodes an MIS instance into the network parameters with no training data and optimizes the relaxed MIS QUBO with Adam. Runtime scales with nodes, not edges. It is reported as competitive with or better than learned MIS methods. It is MIS-specific in its published form. — [arXiv 2406.19532](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.19532v2); [Neural Networks 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0893608022003082)
- The same "dataless" idea has been applied to resource-constrained project scheduling. — [arXiv 2507.05322](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.05322v1)

**Trained-distribution solvers** (poor fit for a custom per-instance energy)
- DIFUSCO (Sun & Yang, NeurIPS 2023), graph diffusion for TSP/MIS. — [GitHub Edward-Sun/DIFUSCO](https://github.com/Edward-Sun/DIFUSCO)
- T2T (NeurIPS 2023) and Fast T2T (NeurIPS 2024), diffusion plus gradient search at test time. — [GitHub Thinklab-SJTU/Fast-T2T](https://github.com/Thinklab-SJTU/Fast-T2T); [Fast T2T paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/352b13f01566ae34affacc60e98c16af-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- GFlowNet-CO ("Let the Flows Tell", graph CO: MIS, MDS, MaxCut, MaxClique). — [arXiv 2305.17010](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.17010)
- Erdős Goes Neural (Karalias & Loukas, NeurIPS 2020), an unsupervised probabilistic-penalty loss that gives certified-quality rounding. It is trained over an instance distribution. — [GitHub Stalence/erdos_neu](https://github.com/Stalence/erdos_neu); [arXiv 2006.10643](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.10643)
- Licenses for these four repositories were not verified.
- Regularized Langevin Dynamics for CO (arXiv 2502.00277) is another sampler-style, instance-wise alternative. — [arXiv 2502.00277](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.00277)

**Critiques and counter-evidence**
- Angelini & Ricci-Tersenghi (Nat. Mach. Intell. 5:29–31, 2023): on MIS, a near-linear-time greedy finds much better solutions than PI-GNN and is about 10⁴× faster at 10⁶ variables. — [arXiv 2206.13211](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.13211); [NMI](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-022-00589-y)
- Boettcher (Nat. Mach. Intell. 5:24–25, 2023): PI-GNN on MaxCut is "only minutely better than gradient descent" and "outperformed by a greedy algorithm". — [arXiv 2210.00623](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.00623); [NMI](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-022-00587-0)
- Schuetz et al.'s replies argue that these are non-representative sparse instances where greedy is expected to do well, and give improved numbers claimed to refute Boettcher. — [Reply to Boettcher](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12096); [Reply to Angelini](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03602)
- FrontierCO (Feng, Sun, Li, Talwalkar, Yang; ICLR 2026) evaluated 16 ML solvers (GNN, neural-symbolic and LLM-agent) on 8 CO problems with competition and industrial instances. It finds "a persistent performance gap [to SOTA classical solvers] that widens under structurally challenging and large instance sizes (e.g., TSP up to 10M nodes; MIS up to 8M)", with ML occasionally winning. — [arXiv 2505.16952](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16952)
- Pro-ML claims: X2GNN reports better solutions than Gurobi on large graphs under "reasonable time budgets", and Meta-EGN reports better approximation than Gurobi 9.5 on hard synthetic instances with a 3000 s Gurobi budget. These are graph problems (MIS etc.), not min-max selection. — [X2GNN ICLR 2025 PDF](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/gomes/pdf/2025_acikalin_iclr_x2gnn.pdf) (claims from the search snippet; [UNVERIFIED] in detail)
- Min & Gomes, "Graph Neural Networks are Heuristics" (arXiv 2601.13465, 2026), argue that beating greedy is not a meaningful test in itself. Their single-pass unsupervised GNN reaches a TSP-100/200/500 gap of 5.55 / 9.18 / 13.36 %, against greedy's 24.8–26.1 % and Christofides' 12–13 %. — [arXiv 2601.13465](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.13465v1)
- Training–test misalignment in unsupervised CO: a lower relaxed training loss does not guarantee better rounded solutions. — [arXiv 2506.16732](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.16732)

### Inferences
- **Fit to our objective.** FEM and QQA optimize any autograd-differentiable energy over relaxed marginals, so they can take the *true* min-max objective via a log-sum-exp smooth max over 144 windows, L_w = C_w − (Dᵀp)_w, plus a budget augmented Lagrangian. They do not need the τ-threshold QUBO surrogate. One step costs one sparse Dᵀp product (nnz ≈ 1.85n) plus its transpose: O(n) per replica. At n = 10⁵ with 64–256 replicas that is well within 24 CPU threads and 123 GB. This is essentially our matrix-free SB, but with a smooth-max energy and entropy annealing in place of Hamiltonian dynamics.
- **Expected quality.** Structurally, the relaxed marginal problem at β → ∞ *is* the LP relaxation, with a smooth max and a penalized budget. These methods are therefore likely to land near z_LP and then face the same rounding problem LP+repair solves in about 1 s. I expect them to match LP+repair at best on the linear objective. Their real value is when the objective gains **non-linear interactions** (per-slot cache-capacity coupling, one-hot admission/prefetch choices, concave savings), where LP needs linearization or fails.
- **PI-GNN.** The graph here is bipartite episode–window, so a GNN parameterization adds little over direct per-variable logits (FEM/QQA/dQNN-style), and the Angelini and Boettcher critiques suggest it will not beat the greedy+repair baseline.
- **DIFUSCO, T2T and GFlowNet** would need a training distribution of our instances and custom decoders. For a single-instance CPU setting they are the wrong tool.

### Gaps
- I found no primary evidence of FEM, QQA, PI-GNN or dQNN on makespan, load balancing, number partitioning, knapsack-with-conflicts or peak minimization against Gurobi, HiGHS or CP-SAT.
- FEM's license and its CPU wall-times at 10⁵ variables for a custom energy were not verified. QQA4CO's post-paper MIPLIB/SCIP results were not verified.
- The licenses of DIFUSCO, Fast-T2T, GFlowNet-CO and erdos_neu were not checked.

## Q3. Concrete recipe(s) for an LNS scheme: select a neighborhood (episodes touching the top-k peak windows), solve a sub-QUBO with SA/SB/p-bits, accept if the true objective improves

### Takeaway
The literature template (LNS-VT, dwave-hybrid's EnergyImpactDecomposer, Kerberos) is: fix most variables, pick a structured subset, re-optimize the subset with an Ising sampler, and accept on true feasibility plus improvement. The published sweet spot is sub-QUBOs of roughly 10³–10⁴ variables. For our problem the neighborhood should be driven by the argmax windows and the LP's fractional core, and the sub-solver should race an exact sub-MILP against the Ising sampler. The Ising sampler is only likely to help once the sub-objective contains quadratic interactions.

### Cited Findings
- Accept only if the candidate is feasible and strictly improves the objective. Subproblem size should be tunable independently of instance size (e.g., V′·T′ in VRP), and the best range was problem-dependent (6k–10k variables for VRP, different for QMKP). — [arXiv 2607.05169](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05169)
- Composable loop primitives: `EnergyImpactDecomposer` (picks variables with the largest energy impact), `RacingBranches` (runs several sub-solvers in parallel and keeps the best), `LoopUntilNoImprovement`, and a classical `TabuSampler` or SA instead of the QPU. — [dwave-hybrid](https://github.com/dwavesystems/dwave-hybrid)
- Decomposing first and then running parallel exact solves on the parts beat monolithic Gurobi on CVRP. — [arXiv 2602.23038](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.23038)

### Recipe (engineering synthesis; inferences built on the sources above and on our AUDIT numbers)
1. **Incumbent.** x⁰ = LP round-down → shared repair, then `ls` SA on the true objective starting from the *repaired* LP solution (the AUDIT notes this start was not run). Keep the loads L_w and the budget slack incrementally.
2. **Neighborhood selection.** Rotate among several operators, adaptive-LNS style:
   - (a) *Peak*: windows W_k = top-k by L_w (k = 3–10, plus windows within ε of the max). N = all episodes touching W_k, both selected and unselected, capped at |N| ≈ 500–5,000 by keeping the highest d_{e,w}/s_e and every currently selected one.
   - (b) *LP-core*: the fractional LP variables plus episodes with |reduced cost| < ε. This is the ≤ 26-variable core plus its near-tight neighbors.
   - (c) *Time block*: a contiguous block of 6–24 windows around the peak, which corresponds to time-block decomposition.
   - (d) *Random swap set*, for diversification.
3. **Subproblem.** With x fixed outside N, the effective loads are L_w^fix, the budget is W_N = W − s_{¬N}ᵀx, and the objective is min max_{w ∈ touched(N)} (L_w^fix − Σ_{e∈N} d_{e,w} x_e) subject to the sub-budget, also bounded by the unchanged max outside N. Build the sub-QUBO with our α/τ surrogate: τ = current peak − δ, α from the loads, μ found by bisection, or a small penalty (P = 0.1·μ_max/W did best in the AUDIT). |N| ≤ 5k keeps an explicit sparse Q small (≤ ~10⁵ nonzeros), so dwave_sa, openjij, MQ DSB or p-bit Gibbs run in well under a second per call, instead of the 18–108 s per full-size call measured in the AUDIT.
4. **Race the sub-solvers** (RacingBranches): (i) HiGHS MILP on the sub-epigraph model (145 rows or fewer, |N| binaries, 1–5 s limit); (ii) SA/SB/p-bit on the sub-QUBO, with repair; (iii) `ls` true-objective SA restricted to N.
5. **Accept** if the true peak drops (strictly, or with a tie-break on budget slack), or use a late-acceptance or SA-style criterion on the true peak for diversification. Update α and τ with the iterative min-max reweighting after each accept.
6. **Stopping and bounds.** z_LP is a certified lower bound, so report gap % of headroom exactly as in the AUDIT.
7. **Where the Ising branch can win.** Once the objective includes cache-capacity coupling per time slot or one-hot admission/prefetch options with pairwise interactions, the sub-MILP needs McCormick or indicator linearization and slows down, while the sub-QUBO takes the quadratic terms natively. That is the regime to test.

### Gaps
- No published LNS uses peak-window neighborhoods for min-max selection with an Ising sub-solver, so the recipe above is unvalidated.
- p-bit sub-solver code: this report did not look for a CPU p-bit library with a sparse API (probabilistic-computing work is GPU/FPGA-oriented); [UNVERIFIED].
- I found no published head-to-head of a sub-MILP against a sub-QUBO inside LNS on a min-max objective. Building one is the experiment to run.
