# Decomposition of large QUBOs vs. classical MILP/LP for budget-constrained min-max selection

Problem under study (P): choose x_e in {0,1} for n = 10^4-10^6 items; item e has cost s_e >= 0 and nonnegative contributions d_{e,w} to m ~ 144-1000 windows, and each item touches only L = a few consecutive windows. The goal is to minimize max_w (C_w - sum_e d_{e,w} x_e) subject to sum_e s_e x_e <= W. As a MILP: min z s.t. sum_e d_{e,w} x_e + z >= C_w for every w, sum_e s_e x_e <= W, x binary, z free.

Verification legend: **[verified]** means I read the claim in the fetched primary source during this session. **[search-snippet]** means it comes only from a search-result summary, and the primary source was not read. **[from memory, unverified]** means a standard bibliographic fact or result that I did not re-check this session. Anything labelled "Inference" is my own reasoning, not a sourced claim.

## Q1. Complexity and approximability (min-max / max-min multi-scenario knapsack, UFP-cover, multi-window)

### Takeaway
(P) is a max-min multi-scenario knapsack in which each window is a scenario, plus per-scenario offsets C_w. With a constant number of windows it is only weakly NP-hard: a pseudo-polynomial DP exists, and the max-min knapsack has an FPTAS. When the number of windows is part of the input, the problem is strongly NP-hard, and the max-min knapsack cannot be approximated within any factor. Your instances have m = 144-1000 windows, so the non-constant regime applies and no useful worst-case multiplicative guarantee exists. Time-locality does not remove NP-hardness, because a single window already contains knapsack. It does, however, make the decision version a generalisation of UFP-cover on a path, which has constant-factor approximations.

### Cited Findings
- The Aissi, Bazgan and Vanderpooten survey ("Min-max and min-max regret versions of combinatorial optimization problems: a survey", EJOR 197(2):427-438, 2009) covers the min-max and min-max regret versions of shortest path, spanning tree, assignment, min cut, min s-t cut and knapsack **[verified: title/abstract]**. Author PDF: [Aissi et al. survey PDF](https://www.lamsade.dauphine.fr/~bazgan/Papers/EJOR09a.pdf). DOI 10.1016/j.ejor.2008.09.012 **[from memory, unverified]**.
- The survey says: "if the number of scenarios is non-constant (the number of scenarios is part of the input), these problems become strongly NP-hard, even when the classical problems are solvable in polynomial time". Its Table 4 lists Knapsack (min-max) as "strongly NP-hard [43]", where [43] is Kouvelis & Yu, *Robust Discrete Optimization and Its Applications*, Kluwer 1997 **[verified]** — [Aissi et al. survey](https://www.lamsade.dauphine.fr/~bazgan/Papers/EJOR09a.pdf)
- For a constant number of scenarios, Table 3 lists Knapsack as "NP-hard, pseudo-poly [43]". The survey adds that the NP-hardness reductions come from PARTITION, which is only weakly NP-hard, so the exact status (weak or strong) is "only partially known" for several problems. For max-min knapsack the pseudo-polynomial DP settles it: the problem is weakly NP-hard **[verified]** — [Aissi et al. survey](https://www.lamsade.dauphine.fr/~bazgan/Papers/EJOR09a.pdf)
- The pseudo-polynomial DP for Discrete Max-Min Knapsack uses the state W_i(v_1..v_k), the minimum weight of a subset of the first i items with profit v_s in scenario s. Its running time is O(n (max_s sum_i p_i^s)^k), exponential in the number of scenarios k. It improves on Yu's (1996) algorithm, which runs in O(n b (max_s sum_i p_i^s)^k) **[verified]** — [Aissi et al. survey](https://www.lamsade.dauphine.fr/~bazgan/Papers/EJOR09a.pdf). Yu, G., "On the max-min 0-1 knapsack problem with robust optimization applications", *Operations Research* 44(2):407-415, 1996 **[from memory, unverified]**.
- Approximation (Table 6, k scenarios): Knapsack min-max has an **FPTAS** for constant k [4], and is **"non approx."** for non-constant k [4]. The min-max *regret* version cannot be approximated even for constant k. The FPTAS follows from Papadimitriou–Yannakakis multi-objective FPTASs, applied via Proposition 11: an f(n)-approximation of the Pareto set gives an f(n)-approximation for min-max **[verified]** — [Aissi et al. survey](https://www.lamsade.dauphine.fr/~bazgan/Papers/EJOR09a.pdf). Reference [4] is Aissi, Bazgan, Vanderpooten, "Approximation of min-max and min-max regret versions of some combinatorial optimization problems", EJOR 179:281-290, 2007 **[from memory, unverified]**.
- The survey also gives a general k-approximation for min-max versions of polynomially solvable problems: optimise the average scenario (Proposition 1). It does not apply directly here, because knapsack itself is NP-hard **[verified]** — [Aissi et al. survey](https://www.lamsade.dauphine.fr/~bazgan/Papers/EJOR09a.pdf)
- A search snippet claimed that max-min knapsack is "strongly NP-hard even when the number of scenarios is fixed" ([search result pointing to ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0305054806002577)). **This contradicts** the survey's pseudo-polynomial DP for fixed k. Pseudo-polynomial solvability rules out strong NP-hardness unless P=NP, so treat that snippet as erroneous.
- Multidimensional (vector) knapsack with a fixed number of constraints has a PTAS due to Frieze and Clarke. There is no EPTAS or FPTAS for dimension >= 2 unless P=NP **[search-snippet]** — [Geometry Meets Vectors, FSTTCS 2022](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol250-fsttcs2022/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2022.23/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2022.23.pdf); [On the PTAS Complexity of Multidimensional Knapsack, ITCS 2026](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.ITCS.2026.50). Frieze & Clarke, EJOR 15(1):100-109, 1984, DOI 10.1016/0377-2217(84)90053-5 **[from memory, unverified]**.
- Grobben, Moura and Yaman (arXiv:2512.02571, rev. Feb 2026) give "the first approximation scheme" (a PTAS) for **covering** MILPs with a fixed number of constraints. For a single constraint they give an FPTAS and an approximate LP formulation, and packing analogues also hold **[verified: abstract]** — [arXiv:2512.02571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02571). For fixed z, the decision version of (P) is exactly such a covering program: m covering rows plus a cost objective.
- UFP-cover on a path is the following problem: a path has a demand on each edge, and tasks have a subpath, a size and a cost; select the cheapest set of tasks so that every edge's demand is covered. The best known polynomial-time ratio is 4 (Bar-Noy et al., STOC 2000). Cristi & Wiese (STACS 2020) give polynomial-time optimal or (1+ε) results under resource augmentation, and a PTAS when edge capacities lie in a constant range **[verified: abstract page]** — [Cristi & Wiese, STACS 2020, DOI 10.4230/LIPIcs.STACS.2020.44](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.STACS.2020.44). A QPTAS for UFP-cover is due to Höhn, Mestre & Wiese (ICALP 2014 / Algorithmica 80(4), 2018) **[search-snippet]** — [Höhn, Mestre, Wiese, ICALP 2014](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-662-43948-7_52). The journal version of Bar-Noy et al. is "A unified approach to approximating resource allocation and scheduling", JACM 48(5), 2001, DOI 10.1145/502102.502107 **[from memory, unverified]**.

### Inferences
- **Mapping.** max_w (C_w - D_w(x)) = -min_w (D_w(x) - C_w). (P) is therefore Discrete Max-Min Knapsack with k = m scenarios and scenario offsets. Setting C_w = 0 recovers standard max-min knapsack, so strong NP-hardness with m in the input transfers directly to general (P).
- **Offsets make multiplicative ratios meaningless.** The optimum can be 0 or negative (full coverage), so ratio guarantees such as FPTAS or "non-approx." are the wrong yardstick. What matters in practice is an additive error, of the form z_heur - z_LP <= (a few item contributions). LP rounding delivers exactly this (Q2).
- **Time-locality.** The Kouvelis–Yu strong-hardness reduction uses items that are active in many scenarios, so it does not obviously survive when each item touches only L consecutive windows. I found no source that settles the complexity of (P) under interval or banded structure. The problem is still at least weakly NP-hard, since m = 1 is plain knapsack. When d_{e,w} is constant across an item's interval, the decision version with fixed z is UFP-cover, which has a 4-approximation. Binary search on z combined with that 4-approximation would give a bicriteria result: exact z with budget blow-up <= 4W. That is not attractive in practice.
- **Constant m.** An FPTAS exists in principle for constant m, but its DP runs in time exponential in m. With m >= 144 it is useless in practice.

### Gaps
- I found no source on the complexity or approximability of max-min knapsack when scenario profiles are banded or interval-structured (items active in <= L consecutive scenarios). This is open or unknown to me.
- I did not confirm whether UFP-cover is strongly NP-hard. I recall that it is, via general caching (Chrobak et al., ESA 2010), but this is unverified.
- The DOIs for Aissi 2009 and 2007, Yu 1996 and Frieze–Clarke 1984 come from memory and were not checked against Crossref.

## Q2. LP relaxation + rounding: basic-solution argument

### Takeaway
The LP relaxation has only m+1 non-bound rows: m window rows plus the budget row. By the standard vertex argument, an optimal basic solution therefore has at most about m fractional x_e, because z is free and occupies one basic slot. Rounding all of them down keeps the budget feasible and raises the peak by at most the d-mass of the fractional items in the worst window. With 10^5-10^6 items and at most 1000 fractional ones (each touching L windows), the gap from the LP bound is typically tiny relative to C_w. LP + rounding is therefore near-optimal *in additive terms* for this structure, although there is no multiplicative guarantee.

### Cited Findings
- The LP-based PTAS for fixed-dimensional knapsack (Frieze & Clarke) relies on the same idea: enumerate a constant number of large items, then solve an LP whose basic solution has few fractional variables **[search-snippet]** — [FSTTCS 2022](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol250-fsttcs2022/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2022.23/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2022.23.pdf); [arXiv:2512.02571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02571)
- Multidimensional knapsack has an LP whose integrality gap is 1+ε after guessing large items **[search-snippet: title]** — [arXiv:1005.3324](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1005.3324)
- Classical statement of the vertex argument: a basic feasible solution of {A x (<=,>=) b, 0 <= x <= 1} with r non-bound rows has at most r variables strictly between their bounds. This underlies Lenstra–Shmoys–Tardos (Math. Programming 46:259-271, 1990, DOI 10.1007/BF01585745) and Kellerer–Pferschy–Pisinger, *Knapsack Problems* (Springer 2004, DOI 10.1007/978-3-540-24777-7), chapter on d-dimensional knapsack **[from memory, unverified]**.
- For max-min multi-scenario knapsack, Taniguchi, Yamada & Kataoka (C&OR 35:2034-2048, 2008) use a surrogate relaxation (aggregating scenarios with weights) plus pegging tests. They report "upper and lower bounds of very high accuracy in a few seconds" and exact B&B on the reduced problem **[search-snippet]** — [ACM DL entry, DOI 10.1016/j.cor.2006.10.002](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1016/j.cor.2006.10.002)

### Inferences
- **Precise count.** The LP is min z s.t. D x + z·1 >= C (m rows), s^T x <= W (1 row), 0 <= x <= 1. A vertex has at most m+1 basic variables among the non-bound constraints. z is basic whenever the objective is interior, which leaves **at most m fractional x_e**, and at most m+1 if z sits at a degenerate position. At n = 10^6 and m = 1000, at most 0.1% of the variables are fractional.
- **Round-down bound.** Let F be the set of fractional items. The rounded solution satisfies z_round <= z_LP + max_w sum_{e in F, e touches w} d_{e,w} x_e^LP <= z_LP + (max number of fractional items on any window) · d_max. The optimum is sandwiched as z_LP <= z_OPT <= z_round, so the additive gap is certified per instance at no extra cost. When individual d_{e,w} are small relative to C_w (many small items per window), the gap is negligible.
- **Better than pure round-down.** After fixing all integral LP values, the at most m fractional items and the slack budget W - s^T x_int form a small residual max-min problem (at most about 1000 binaries). A MILP solver closes it in seconds, or it can be filled greedily: add items in order of Δpeak reduction per cost until the budget is exhausted. This is "relax-and-fix" or "LP-and-fix".
- **Where it can fail.** If a few items carry d_{e,w} comparable to C_w (lumpy items), the additive bound is loose. Enumerate or branch on those large items first (Frieze–Clarke style), then run the LP on the rest.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that analyses LP rounding specifically for max-min knapsack with m scenarios in the "many small items" regime. The additive bound above is my own derivation from the standard vertex argument.
- I found no empirical study of LP-gap magnitudes for banded or interval-structured instances.

## Q3. Lagrangian relaxation, column generation, and practical MILP/LP solver scale

### Takeaway
**Correction to the premise:** dualizing only the *budget* does **not** decompose (P) into per-item decisions, because the inner problem still has the max over windows coupling all items. The decomposing relaxation is to dualize the **window constraints** with multipliers μ on the simplex. This yields a single 0/1 knapsack with profits p_e = sum_w μ_w d_{e,w}. Further dualizing the budget with λ gives the per-item threshold rule x_e = 1 iff p_e > λ s_e. The best μ can be found by subgradient or bundle methods, or equivalently by the LP (Q2). Given that the LP has only about 1000 rows, solving it directly with HiGHS or PDLP is simpler than a hand-rolled Lagrangian and at least as tight.

### Cited Findings
- Surrogate relaxation (weighted aggregation of scenarios) plus pegging is the established approach for max-min multi-scenario knapsack **[search-snippet]** — [Taniguchi, Yamada, Kataoka 2008](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1016/j.cor.2006.10.002)
- An exact method based on Dantzig–Wolfe reformulation, column generation and branch-and-bound exists for the multi-scenario max-min knapsack **[search-snippet]** — [ResearchGate: "Solving the Multiscenario Max-Min Knapsack Problem Exactly with Column Generation and Branch-and-Bound"](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/276366728_Solving_the_Multiscenario_Max-Min_Knapsack_Problem_Exactly_with_Column_Generation_and_Branch-and-Bound). Authors and venue not verified.
- Other heuristics for max-min knapsack: hybrid approaches for the two-scenario case ([Hanafi et al., ITOR 2012](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1475-3995.2011.00836.x)); an iterative algorithm ([Operational Research, Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12351-019-00463-7)); ML-assisted methods ([Computers & Industrial Engineering 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0360835224001050)). All **[search-snippet]**.
- **Mittelmann MIPLIB2017 benchmark, 7 July 2026, 240 instances.** Scaled shifted geometric mean (lower is better) and instances solved: COPT-8.0.3 1.0 (219 solved); Optverse-2.0.1 1.72 (210); XSMOO 5.15 (174); HiGHS-1.15.1 parallel 5.44 (179); SCIPCO 6.59 (153); HiGHS-1.15.0 7.55 (158); SCIP-10 9.93 (136). Gurobi and CPLEX are not included **[verified: page fetch summary]** — [plato.asu.edu/ftp/milp.html](https://plato.asu.edu/ftp/milp.html)
- Gurobi withdrew from Mittelmann's benchmarks in August 2024, and MindOpt followed in December **[search-snippet, secondary blog]** — [Ceris blog](https://ceris.fyi/blog/mip-solver-comparison-2025/). Current public head-to-head numbers for Gurobi are therefore unavailable.
- **PDLP** (primal-dual hybrid gradient with presolve, diagonal preconditioning, adaptive restarts and feasibility polishing; C++ in OR-Tools, multithreaded) was evaluated on 11 LPs with 125M-6.3B nonzeros and solved 8 of them to a 1% gap within 6 days on one machine. An internal distributed version has solved LPs with up to 92B nonzeros **[search-snippet of abstract]** — [Applegate et al., arXiv:2501.07018](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.07018); [Math. Prog. Computation 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12532-026-00309-2); [Google Research blog](https://research.google/blog/scaling-up-linear-programming-with-pdlp/)

### Inferences
- **Size of (P) as an LP/MILP.** It has n+1 columns, m+1 ≈ 145-1001 rows, and nnz ≈ n·L + n. With L ≈ 3 that is 4×10^6 nonzeros at n = 10^6. This is *small* for a simplex or IPM code: the basis is only (m+1)×(m+1). Dual simplex or IPM in HiGHS, Gurobi or COPT should solve the LP in seconds to around a minute. PDLP is unnecessary at this size, and a first-order method's modest accuracy would blur the vertex structure that the rounding relies on. **(Timing is an unbenchmarked estimate.)**
- **MILP.** With 10^6 binaries and about 1000 rows, the root LP is cheap, and solver primal heuristics (RINS, rounding, feasibility pump) should find a solution near the LP bound quickly, for the reason given in Q2. *Proving* optimality may take long: min-max objectives with many near-identical items produce huge symmetric plateaus. The practical approach is to set a time limit and a relative-gap target (for example 0.1%) and report the LP bound. A good solution is expected within minutes, but proven optimality at the top of the range may never arrive **(unverified; needs a pilot run)**.
- **CP-SAT** needs integer coefficients and handles 10^6 Booleans with long weighted linear sums less naturally than an LP-based MILP solver. I expect it to be weaker here than HiGHS/Gurobi with LP bounding **(inference, unverified)**.
- **Lagrangian over window multipliers μ.** The dual function g(μ) = sum_w μ_w C_w - max{ sum_e p_e(μ) x_e : s^T x <= W } requires one knapsack (or greedy LP-knapsack) solve per iteration, which is O(n log n). The bound equals the LP bound when the inner knapsack is LP-relaxed. This is worth building only as a warm-start or price heuristic for a custom streaming implementation (for example an online admission variant), not as the primary offline solver.
- **Column generation** (columns = feasible knapsack selections, master = choose a convex combination minimising the max) is the right exact tool only when m is small and n is huge. For m ≈ 1000, a direct MILP is simpler.

### Gaps
- I found no published benchmark of HiGHS, Gurobi or CP-SAT on max-min knapsack instances with n = 10^5-10^6 and m ≈ 1000. The scale claims above are estimates that need a pilot run.
- Current Gurobi-vs-HiGHS numbers on public benchmarks are unavailable because Gurobi withdrew.
- I could not access the full Taniguchi et al. paper (ScienceDirect returned 403). Instance sizes and scenario counts therefore remain unreported.

## Q4. QUBO decomposition: QBSolv, D-Wave hybrid, rolling horizon, sparsification, persistency

### Takeaway
Existing QUBO decomposition tools were validated only at about 10^3 variables (QBSolv: 2500-variable instances). QBSolv has been deprecated since 2021/2022. The QUBO *encoding* of (P) is itself the bottleneck. The budget penalty (s^T x + slack - W)^2 is fully dense, about n^2/2 couplers: 5×10^11 at n = 10^6, and about 5×10^9 even at n = 10^5. Every window penalty adds a dense clique over its items, and z plus slack variables need binary expansions. Persistency and roof duality work on sparse, structured QUBOs but are unlikely to fix much in penalty-dense QUBOs. D-Wave's CQM hybrid avoids the dense penalty by taking linear constraints natively, but it caps at 500k variables. QUBO is strictly worse per engineering hour than LP/MILP for this problem.

### Cited Findings
- **QBSolv** (Booth, Reinhardt, Roy, D-Wave Technical Report 14-1006A-A, 2017-01-09) partitions a QUBO into subQUBOs and solves each with the other variables clamped to the incumbent. SubQUBOs are chosen as contiguous blocks of an "impact"-sorted index, where impact is the objective increase from flipping a bit. Each round is followed by a global tabu search. The subQUBO size was "about 45 variables on a 1000-qubit D-Wave 2X". The fraction of variables re-optimised per pass was typically 0.05-0.15 **[verified]** — [QBSolv tech report](https://www.dwavequantum.com/media/jhlpvult/partitioning_qubos_for_quantum_acceleration-2.pdf)
- QBSolv was benchmarked on ORLIB/Beasley instances "up to 2500 variables with significant density". It matched the best-known values of Wang et al. and was "almost twice as fast" as the best previous implementation, using a *classical* tabu subsolver rather than the QPU **[verified]** — [QBSolv tech report](https://www.dwavequantum.com/media/jhlpvult/partitioning_qubos_for_quantum_acceleration-2.pdf)
- QBSolv was deprecated at the end of 2021, with support discontinued after March 2022. D-Wave directs users to dwave-hybrid or the Leap hybrid solvers **[search-snippet]** — [dwavesystems/qbsolv GitHub](https://github.com/dwavesystems/qbsolv)
- **dwave-hybrid Kerberos** is an asynchronous decomposition sampler that runs tabu search, simulated annealing and QPU subproblem sampling in parallel on high-energy-impact variables. Its default traversal is BFS in Kerberos and energy-ordered in EnergyImpactDecomposer **[search-snippet]** — [kerberos.py](https://github.com/dwavesystems/dwave-hybrid/blob/master/hybrid/reference/kerberos.py); [dwave-hybrid docs](https://docs.ocean.dwavesys.com/en/latest/docs_hybrid/intro/using.html); [D-Wave community thread](https://support.dwavesys.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/360043683354-Behavior-of-dwave-hybrid-s-Kerberos)
- **Leap CQM hybrid solver limits:** 500,000 variables, 100,000 constraints, 750,000,000 total biases (linear + quadratic), 15,000,000 variables with real-valued quadratic interactions. It supports binary, integer and real variables and native linear constraints **[search-snippet of D-Wave docs]** — [CQM solver properties](https://docs.dwavequantum.com/en/latest/industrial_optimization/solver_cqm_properties.html); [CQM parameters](https://docs.dwavequantum.com/en/latest/industrial_optimization/solver_cqm_parameters.html)
- Hybrid decomposition for larger-than-QPU *lattice-structured* problems exists (ACM TQC). It targets structured sparse problems **[search-snippet: title]** — [ACM TQC DOI 10.1145/3579368](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3579368)
- **Roof duality and persistency preprocessing (PrePro)**, by Boros, Hammer & Tavares, combines roof-duality persistencies, decomposition and probing, and runs in polynomial time. It reports 100% data reduction on MAX-CUT from VLSI, MAX-CLIQUE on c-fat graphs, and vertex cover on random planar graphs with up to 500,000 vertices **[search-snippet]** — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Preprocessing-of-unconstrained-quadratic-binary-Boros-Hammer/e589f4a3f298bb0522a33628b0d0b0a4c6133c8d); [academia.edu copy](https://www.academia.edu/75967301/Preprocessing_of_unconstrained_quadratic_binary_optimization). Published in Discrete Applied Math, circa 2008 **[from memory, unverified]**. A scalable QPBO implementation for very large graphs is also available — [Skielex/QPBO](https://github.com/Skielex/QPBO)
- **Inequality constraints in QUBO.** Slack-variable plus quadratic-penalty encodings add qubits and "distort the optimization landscape". Unbalanced penalization (Montañez-Barrera et al., Quantum Sci. Technol. 2024) avoids slack variables but can create "false minima", where the QUBO minimum is not the constrained optimum **[search-snippet]** — [arXiv:2211.13914](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.13914); [IOP DOI 10.1088/2058-9565/ad35e4](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/2058-9565/ad35e4); [Slack-free penalty, arXiv:2504.12611](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.12611v1)
- Independent evaluations report strong classical performance of QBSolv-style decomposition on "quantum-ready" formulations, and constrained classical formulations doing better on graph partitioning **[search-snippet: titles]** — [arXiv:2005.11294](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.11294); [arXiv:2006.15067](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.15067); [Evaluation of Quantum and Hybrid Solvers, arXiv:2403.10455](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.10455)

### Inferences (encoding-size arithmetic is my own)
- **QUBO form of (P).** Minimise z_bin + A·Σ_w (C_w - z_bin - D_w x + slack_w)^2 + B·(s^T x + slack_B - W)^2. Here z_bin is a K-bit expansion of z, and each slack needs about log2(range) bits.
  - Budget term: n(n-1)/2 couplers, about 5×10^11 at n = 10^6 and about 5×10^9 at n = 10^5. The CQM 750M-bias cap is exceeded at n ≈ 38.7k if the budget is written as a penalty.
  - Window terms: with about n·L/m items per window (≈ 3,000 at n = 10^6, m = 1000, L = 3), about 4.5M couplers per window, or about 4.5×10^9 in total, plus couplings of every window to the shared z bits.
  - The shared z bits connect to *every* item, so they create a hub that defeats locality-based partitioning.
- **Consequence for decomposers.** QBSolv, Kerberos and EnergyImpact clamp everything outside a small subQUBO. With a global dense budget penalty, every subproblem's linear term depends on the global budget residual. That is manageable in principle, since it is a single scalar, but the penalty weights A and B must dominate the objective. This produces ill-conditioned energy landscapes where local moves that add an item cross large penalty barriers. The known result is that penalty QUBOs of knapsack-type constraints are hard for local search.
- **Time-block rolling horizon / large-neighbourhood search** is the one decomposition that fits the structure well: items touch few consecutive windows, so blocks of windows interact only at their boundaries and through the global budget. It works equally well, and better, with a MILP subsolver on each block. Allocate budget W_b across blocks by using the LP dual price λ of the budget row, or fix the rest of the incumbent and re-solve a block of windows (LNS/RINS). Once the problem is decomposed this way, the QUBO subsolver adds nothing: each block is a small MILP.
- **Sparsification** (dropping small couplers) is not applicable to the budget penalty without changing feasibility semantics. Dropping budget-penalty couplers silently allows budget violation.
- **Persistency.** The 500k-vertex successes are on sparse graph problems with natural submodular structure. For penalty QUBOs from equality or inequality constraints I found no evidence of high fix rates. I expect that roof duality would fix few variables here **(unverified)**. The LP reduced-cost test is the natural classical analogue of pegging: fix x_e = 0 or 1 when |reduced cost| exceeds the gap. As in Taniguchi et al., it is cheaper and stronger here.
- **QPU-native size.** SubQUBOs of about 45 variables (2X era), or a few hundred on current Advantage-class hardware **(unverified)**, would require thousands of subproblem calls per pass at n = 10^6.

### Gaps
- I found no published run of QBSolv, dwave-hybrid or any QUBO decomposer at 10^5-10^6 variables with dense constraint penalties. The only verified size is 2500 variables (QBSolv).

## Q5. Practical recommendation for time-local items and one global budget

### Takeaway
Use a classical LP/MILP pipeline. It gives certified near-optimality through the LP lower bound, handles 10^6 items in minutes on commodity hardware (estimate, to be confirmed by a pilot), and takes on the order of a day of engineering. Any QUBO route needs large extra effort (penalty tuning, slack and z encodings, and a decomposer that can handle more than about 10^4 variables), gives no optimality certificate, and has no published evidence of matching quality at this scale.

### Cited Findings
- The LP structure makes vertex solutions almost integral (at most m fractional values; see Q2). Mittelmann 2026 shows open-source HiGHS solving a majority of MIPLIB (179/240 parallel), and commercial COPT solving 219/240 — [Mittelmann MILP](https://plato.asu.edu/ftp/milp.html)
- Max-min multi-scenario knapsack is solved well in practice by surrogate or Lagrangian bounds, pegging and B&B — [Taniguchi et al. 2008](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1016/j.cor.2006.10.002)
- QUBO decomposition tools have been validated only at about 2.5×10^3 variables — [QBSolv report](https://www.dwavequantum.com/media/jhlpvult/partitioning_qubos_for_quantum_acceleration-2.pdf). The hybrid CQM service caps at 5×10^5 variables — [D-Wave CQM docs](https://docs.dwavequantum.com/en/latest/industrial_optimization/solver_cqm_properties.html)

### Inferences (recommended pipeline, in order of payoff per engineering hour)
1. **Baseline, about 1 hour of work.** Build the LP with HiGHS (via highspy, or OR-Tools MathOpt) and use dual simplex. Record z_LP as the lower bound.
2. **Round and repair, about 2 hours.** Fix the integral values and round the at most m fractional ones down. Then fill greedily by marginal peak reduction per cost, or solve the residual problem of at most about 1000 binaries as a MILP. Report the certified gap z_round - z_LP.
3. **MILP polish, optional.** Run the full MILP with a time limit and gap target, warm-started with step 2. Before this step, apply LP reduced-cost pegging to cut n drastically.
4. **Beyond about 10^6 items, or for streaming/online use.** Decompose by blocks of windows (rolling horizon or LNS) with the budget split by the dual price λ. Or use the μ-Lagrangian threshold rule x_e = [Σ_w μ_w d_{e,w} > λ s_e], where μ and λ are taken from the LP duals. This is an O(n) admission rule, which directly suits an online "peak-aware admission" setting.
5. **QUBO/Ising, only as a research comparison.** If needed, use a CQM with native constraints on a subsample of up to 5×10^5 items, or a QUBO on single time blocks of about 10^3-10^4 items. Compare against the LP bound from step 1, which gives an honest optimality gap.

- Summary judgement: for this structure the classical MILP/LP approach is **simpler and at least as good**. It is very likely better, because it provides a bound and an almost integral LP. This is an inference from the structural argument, and I found no head-to-head benchmark on this exact problem.

### Gaps
- There is no head-to-head empirical comparison of QUBO or hybrid solvers vs. MILP on max-min knapsack or peak-minimisation at 10^5-10^6 scale. The recommendation rests on structural arguments plus the limits documented above.
- LP solve times at n = 10^6, m = 1000 are estimates. A pilot on real Baleen-derived instances is needed.
